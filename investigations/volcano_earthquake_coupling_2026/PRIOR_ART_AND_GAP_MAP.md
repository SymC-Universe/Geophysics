# Earthquake-Volcano Coupling and Planetary Stabilization: Prior-Art and Gap Map

**Status:** A0 / P0-N exploratory synthesis, NOT FROZEN  
**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**GOM:** v0.8.8  
**Purpose:** Test whether the older planetary-stabilization literature supports a scientifically usable coupled earthquake-volcano research program, identify where the claim breaks, and define gaps suitable for a preregistered tool investigation.

## 1. Historical source candidate

A strong candidate for the paper previously remembered by the investigator is:

**S. de la Cruz-Reyna (1991), "Volcanic eruptions; energy and size," Earthquakes & Volcanoes (USGS), 22(3), 140-141.**

The USGS abstract explicitly describes:
- a balance between energy stored/generated in Earth's interior and heat lost to space;
- planetary-scale self-regulating convection mechanisms;
- volcanism as a mechanism that transports energy to the surface in rapid bursts;
- the expectation that some law/principle constrains how volcanic bursts fit the overall terrestrial energy-transfer balance.

This is genuine prior art for a planetary heat-regulation framing.

**Important limit:** the article does not by itself establish that earthquakes and volcanoes form a coordinated homeostatic pair, nor does it define a measurable stability coordinate.

## 2. Three distinct meanings of "stabilization"

These must not be conflated.

### S1. Interior energy / mechanical-energy regulation

Relevant questions:
- How are tectonic work, elastic strain, gravitational potential energy, magmatic heat, hydrothermal heat, frictional dissipation, seismic radiation, and surface work partitioned?
- Are earthquakes and volcanic/magmatic systems coupled sinks in a control-volume energy budget?
- Does the partition change systematically with loading/state?

Modern anchor:
- Cooke et al. (2025), *What Is the Energy Budget of Subduction Zone Hazards?*, Reviews of Geophysics.
- The framework explicitly includes tectonic work, heat, gravitational potential energy, deformation, earthquake dissipation, magmatic/volcanic energy, landslides and tsunami terms in one subduction-zone control volume.
- The paper states that these components are conventionally treated separately and that important transfers remain poorly constrained.

### S2. Climate / habitability buffering

Relevant questions:
- Does tectonics create weatherable material/topography while volcanism supplies greenhouse gases?
- Can weathering-temperature feedback buffer climate over geologic timescales?
- Under which tectonic modes does buffering fail?

Modern anchors:
- Lenardic et al. (2016), climate-tectonic coupling.
- Carbonate-silicate-cycle literature.
- Interior/habitability reviews.

**Important limit:** climate buffering over millions to billions of years is not evidence that individual earthquakes/eruptions stabilize the planet on event timescales.

### S3. Earthquake-volcano dynamic coupling

Relevant questions:
- When do tectonic earthquakes trigger unrest or eruption?
- Are common changes in volcanic and seismic activity caused by direct triggering or by a shared driver/state?
- Does a volcano's pre-existing state determine response to external stress perturbation?

Modern anchors:
- Seropian et al. (2021), earthquake-triggered eruption review.
- Jenkins et al. (2021), global decadal earthquake-eruption correlations.
- Nishimura (2018) and related catalog studies.

**Current result from prior art:** direct earthquake triggering exists but is uncommon and typically requires a primed volcanic system. Global earthquake-eruption covariation is not explained by direct triggering alone.

## 3. New energy-budget evidence relevant to the old idea

### 3.1 Subduction zones are explicitly treated as coupled energy systems

Cooke et al. (2025) provides a modern cross-disciplinary energy-budget framework in which stored crustal energy is released through multiple processes including earthquakes and volcanic activity.

This supports asking a coupled-system question.

It does **not** demonstrate:
- a universal attractor;
- a homeostatic target;
- a preferred damping ratio;
- a fixed quake-to-volcano partition;
- causality between earthquake rate and eruption rate.

### 3.2 Arc heat loss is not eruption-only

Ingebritsen et al. (2026), *Arc Heat Flow and Magmatic Heat Budgets*, shows that arc heat can leave through:
- eruptive products;
- geothermal conduction;
- fumaroles/steam;
- thermal springs;
- slightly heated groundwater.

**Consequence:** any "volcanic release" variable based only on eruptions is physically incomplete.

### 3.3 Earthquake energy accounting remains nontrivial

Standard earthquake energy partitions include:
- seismic radiation;
- frictional heating;
- fracture/breakdown work;
- elastic/internal-energy change;
- gravitational-potential changes under some formulations.

The literature contains large estimates for earthquake-associated gravitational-energy changes, but their interpretation as a major contribution to planetary heat flow is not a settled ordinary seismological result.

**Consequence:** do not build a planetary-stability claim on a single energy-accounting convention. Competing budgets must be carried as model alternatives.

## 4. Candidate scientific holes

### G1. No closed observational quake-volcano energy budget

The modern subduction-zone energy-budget literature explicitly identifies missing/poorly constrained transfers. A practical observational closure across:
- tectonic loading,
- seismic release,
- magma storage/transport,
- hydrothermal heat loss,
- deformation,
- eruption,
- recovery
has not been established as a routine tool.

### G2. Direct triggering does not explain global covariance

Global earthquake and volcanic activity covary on some timescales, but known direct triggering is too rare to explain the full signal.

Open alternatives:
- common mantle/plate forcing;
- stress-transfer cascades;
- observation/catalog artifacts;
- long-memory clustering in each process;
- climate/hydrologic forcing;
- coincidental nonstationarity;
- genuine coupled geodynamic state.

This is a strong falsifiable target.

### G3. "Primed volcano" is scientifically meaningful but not a universal state variable

Triggering reviews identify important volcanic conditions such as:
- magma viscosity;
- open versus closed degassing;
- hydrothermal-system state;
- magma availability/pressure;
- deformation and seismic unrest.

There is no evidence yet that one universal scalar adequately represents all of them.

### G4. Cross-timescale coupling is unresolved

The system spans:
- seconds-minutes: rupture, tremor, explosions;
- days-months: swarms, inflation, degassing, postseismic response;
- years-decades: magma recharge, earthquake cycles;
- Myr-Gyr: mantle cooling, volatile cycling, climate buffering.

A broad-use architecture must show which relations persist across scale and which do not.

### G5. Common-driver versus causal-trigger discrimination

A correlation between quake and eruption rates does not identify directionality.

Needed controls:
- local versus remote earthquake windows;
- matched non-eruptive volcanoes;
- matched non-volcanic tectonic segments;
- background clustering/null models;
- ETAS-like seismic nulls;
- eruption renewal/non-Poisson nulls;
- tectonic setting and arc-segment controls.

### G6. Non-eruptions are essential negative evidence

A tool must include:
- volcanoes with strong unrest but no eruption;
- large earthquakes with no volcanic response;
- eruptions without unusual regional tectonic earthquakes;
- closed versus open degassing systems;
- hydrothermal-dominated unrest.

Otherwise the architecture will be selected on positive cases.

### G7. Heat transport is broader than eruption output

Hydrothermal systems may dominate magmatic heat loss in some arcs. Therefore:
- thermal springs,
- fumaroles,
- diffuse degassing,
- hydrothermal discharge
must be considered where available.

### G8. "Stability" needs an operational target

Candidate operational meanings must be tested separately:
- bounded stored energy;
- bounded deformation rate;
- recovery after perturbation;
- persistence of flux balance;
- avoidance of runaway thermal/pressure accumulation;
- return to a statistical operating region;
- preservation of function/habitability on long timescales.

No one definition is privileged prospectively.

## 5. Candidate tool opportunity

The current strongest opportunity is not "prove earthquakes and volcanoes stabilize Earth."

It is:

> Build a transparent geodynamic-state architecture that can adjudicate whether a tectonic-volcanic system is best described by loading, release, transport, recovery, or unresolved/multimode behavior; quantify cross-process coupling where supported; refuse unsupported reduction; and test whether the combined architecture adds information beyond standard earthquake and volcano monitoring products.

Potential layers:
1. tectonic loading/deformation;
2. earthquake release and postseismic recovery;
3. volcanic seismicity/tremor;
4. deformation/inflation-deflation;
5. gas/thermal/hydrothermal flux;
6. eruption/extrusion;
7. network/spatial coupling;
8. explicit uncertainty and refusal.

Lower-case chi is permitted only where a native second-order damping coordinate is physically licensed.

Broader Chi is a candidate system architecture, not a guaranteed scalar.

## 6. Better research questions

### VQ1. Perturbation response
Do volcanoes with independently measured priming state respond differently to comparable regional earthquake stress perturbations?

### VQ2. Common-driver test
After accounting for temporal clustering and catalog structure, does earthquake-volcano covariance contain residual structure consistent with a shared geodynamic state?

### VQ3. Energy-partition architecture
Within a subduction control volume, do seismic, deformation, volcanic, gas and hydrothermal outputs show reproducible trade-offs or coordinated regime shifts?

### VQ4. Unrest without eruption
Can the architecture distinguish recoverable unrest from progression to eruption without requiring a universal scalar?

### VQ5. Eruption without earthquake trigger
Can the same architecture describe eruption progression when no unusual tectonic trigger is present?

### VQ6. Cross-domain transfer
Does the earthquake-side representation-adjudication machinery transfer to volcano monitoring data while preserving volcano-native quantities and refusal states?

### VQ7. Long-term regulation
At geological timescales, can observed/modelled tectonic-volcanic-weathering feedbacks be described as bounded regulation without invoking purpose or teleology?

## 7. Falsification consequences

The planetary-stability hypothesis should be weakened or retired if:
- earthquake-volcano covariance vanishes under appropriate clustering/common-driver controls;
- energy outputs show no reproducible cross-process organization;
- native volcano monitoring variables fully dominate any proposed architecture;
- transfer across volcanoes fails without retuning;
- "stability" changes meaning from case to case;
- the architecture only works after selecting eruptions and excluding non-events.

A useful volcano-monitoring tool could still survive even if planetary homeostasis does not.
